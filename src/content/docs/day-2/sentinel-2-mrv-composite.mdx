This manual turns the Sentinel-2 compositing workflow into an annual product for MRV (measurement, reporting and verification). The design follows four rules:

- avoid the monsoon months, when there are too few clear optical images;
- keep both composite windows inside the reporting year;
- export only the bands used for training;
- write one Int16 image per year, with the season in each band name.

<Callout type="mistake" title="Assets you need">
The script reads Bhutan's boundary from `projects/bhutan-hfld-readiness-training/assets/Bhutan` and exports to the same project. If you don't have access, replace the boundary with your own asset (or `ee.FeatureCollection('USDOS/LSIB_SIMPLE/2017').filter(ee.Filter.eq('country_na', 'Bhutan'))`) and change `assetId` to a folder in your own Cloud project.
</Callout>

## Why this method is used for Bhutan MRV

Optical data in Bhutan are strongly seasonal. The dry and shoulder months give enough clear Sentinel-2 images to build stable composites; the monsoon does not. For MRV, the goal is not to use as many months as possible, but a **repeatable annual product that is consistent from one reporting year to the next**.

The manual counted the available images per month (training example):

| Month | Images | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| January | 64 | strong support |
| February | 46 | strong support |
| March | 54 | strong support |
| April | 40 | usable support |
| May | 19 | weak support |
| June | 1 | do not use for an optical composite |
| July | 1 | do not use for an optical composite |
| August | — (not reported) | very weak support |
| September | 15 | weak support |
| October | 31 | usable support |
| November | 73 | very strong support |
| December | 105 | very strong support |

So the windows are **January–April** and **October–December**. Both stay inside the reporting year, which keeps MRV bookkeeping simple. May to September is left out because it is too cloudy to support reliable training data from year to year. A single March–October composite would mix good months with nearly empty ones and make the model unstable.

**The product:** one image per reporting year, with 12 bands: six Sentinel-2 surface-reflectance bands (B2, B3, B4, B8, B11, B12) for January–April, and the same six for October–December, stored as Int16, with no quality bands.

## Step 3: Cloud Score+

Scene cloud cover only removes very cloudy scenes. Pixel-level masking removes thin cloud, haze and cloud edges. [Cloud Score+](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/GOOGLE_CLOUD_SCORE_PLUS_V1_S2_HARMONIZED) gives each Sentinel-2 pixel a score from 0 (not clear) to 1 (clear). `linkCollection` attaches the matching score image to each Sentinel-2 image, and pixels with `cs_cdf` below 0.60 are masked.

<Callout type="implication">

</Callout>

## Step 4: TDOM shadow cleaning

**TDOM** (temporal dark outlier mask) finds pixels that are much darker in near infrared and SWIR than usual for that place. It compares each image with the mean and standard deviation over the previous three years. A pixel is masked as shadow when both its NIR and SWIR-1 z-scores are below −1.8 and their sum is below 0.20 reflectance. The mask is shrunk by 1.5 pixels to drop isolated pixels, then grown by 2.5 pixels to cover shadow edges. TDOM only updates the mask; no z-score or mask bands are kept.

<CodeBlock script="day-2/sentinel-2-mrv-composite.js" lines={[61, 101]} />

<Callout type="implication">
Training implication: deep terrain and cloud shadows can look like water, bare soil or disturbance. TDOM reduces that confusion before the model is trained.
</Callout>

## Step 5: Terrain correction

A slope facing the sun looks brighter than the same land cover on a shaded slope. The correction computes the **illumination condition** (IC), how directly each pixel faces the sun, from the slope and aspect of the [SRTM](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/USGS_SRTMGL1_003) DEM and the image's sun angles. For each band, a regression of reflectance on IC (over slopes of at least 5°) gives a correction factor *c*, and the band is adjusted:

<Theory title="The C-correction">
$IC = \cos Z \cos S + \sin Z \sin S \cos(\phi_{sun} - \phi_{aspect})$, where $Z$ is the solar zenith angle and $S$ the slope. With $\rho = a \cdot IC + b$ fitted per band and $c = b/a$, the corrected reflectance is $\rho_{corr} = \rho \, \dfrac{\cos S \cos Z + c}{IC + c}$. Pixels facing the sun are darkened, shaded pixels brightened, and flat ground barely changes.
</Theory>

## Notes for using the product

- Use the 12-band image as a **two-season feature stack**: one early-year and one late-year view of the same reporting year. This keeps seasonality instead of averaging it away.
- Keep the **same windows** and the **same band order** every year, so training data and code stay comparable.
- Don't add QA bands to the final asset unless an experiment needs them.
- If one season is poorly covered in a year, **flag it** instead of quietly widening the window.

## Key functions

| Function | What it does |
|---|---|
| `ee.Date.fromYMD(y, m, d).advance(1, 'day')` | Builds dates; the day after the window's last day is the exclusive end |
| `collection.linkCollection(other, bands)` | Adds bands from the matching image of another collection |
| `img.updateMask(condition)` | Masks pixels where the condition is false |
| `collection.reduce(ee.Reducer.stdDev())` | Per-pixel standard deviation over time (for TDOM) |
| `focal_min()`, `focal_max()` | Shrink and grow a mask |
| `ee.Algorithms.Terrain(dem)` | Slope, aspect and hillshade from a DEM |
| `ee.Reducer.linearFit()` | Fits y = scale·x + offset over a region |
| `image.toInt16()` | Converts to 16-bit integers |
| `Export.image.toAsset({...})` | Writes an image to an Earth Engine asset |

## Common mistakes

- **Building a March–October composite.** It mixes usable and unusable months.
- **Exporting IC, TDOM mask, z-score or Cloud Score bands.** Keep the training asset compact.
- **Letting the windows cross calendar years** when the product is used for MRV reporting.
- **Skipping a visual check before exporting.** Bhutan's terrain makes artefacts likely: look at each preview layer.
- **Mixing Level-1C and Level-2A** in one workflow.
