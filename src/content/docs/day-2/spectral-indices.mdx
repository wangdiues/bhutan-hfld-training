A **spectral index** combines two or more bands into one number that highlights a property of the surface, such as green vegetation or water. Indices are among the most useful <Term id="covariate">covariates</Term> for classifying forest on Day 3. This exercise builds five of them from Sentinel-2. Data: [Sentinel-2 surface reflectance (harmonized)](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/COPERNICUS_S2_SR_HARMONIZED).

| Index | Formula (Sentinel-2 bands) | Highlights |
|---|---|---|
| NDVI | (NIR − Red) / (NIR + Red) = (B8 − B4) / (B8 + B4) | green vegetation <Cite id="tucker1979" /> |
| NDWI | (Green − NIR) / (Green + NIR) = (B3 − B8) / (B3 + B8) | open water |
| NDMI | (NIR − SWIR-1) / (NIR + SWIR-1) = (B8 − B11) / (B8 + B11) | water in leaves and soil moisture |
| EVI | 2.5 × (NIR − Red) / (NIR + 6 Red − 7.5 Blue + 1) | vegetation, less saturated in dense forest |
| SAVI | 1.5 × (NIR − Red) / (NIR + Red + 0.5) | vegetation where soil shows through |

## 1.1 NDVI from one image

Load one Sentinel-2 image of western Bhutan (9 January 2024). Select the near-infrared (B8) and red (B4) bands and compute NDVI. Then define how to show NDVI (−1 to 1, blue–white–green), a true-colour view (red, green, blue: B4, B3, B2, values 0–3000) and a SWIR–NIR–red view (B11, B8, B4, with a gamma of 1.4), which is good for looking at vegetation. Add the three layers and print NDVI to the Console.

<Callout type="why">
Why this matters: in the NDVI layer, forest is dark green (0.6 to 0.9), farmland and grassland paler, bare ground and settlements near 0 or white, and rivers blue (below 0). Click around with the Inspector to see the values.
</Callout>

The same NDVI in one line: `s2img.normalizedDifference(['B8', 'B4'])`. It computes (first − second) / (first + second) and is the usual way to write any normalised difference.

## 1.3 NDVI minimum, median and maximum over a year

One image shows one day. To describe a whole year, compute NDVI for every image and reduce the stack. Filter the collection to Thimphu, 2024 and less than 5% cloud. Write NDVI as a **function** that takes an image and returns it with an `NDVI` band added, `map()` it over the collection, then reduce: minimum, median and maximum for each pixel.

<Tabs>
  <TabItem label="JavaScript (Code Editor)">
    <CodeBlock script="day-2/spectral-indices.js" lines={[73, 106]} />
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Python (geemap)">
```python
aoi = ee.Geometry.Point([89.64, 27.47])
s2_collection = (
    ee.ImageCollection("COPERNICUS/S2_SR_HARMONIZED")
    .filterBounds(aoi)
    .filterDate("2024-01-01", "2024-12-31")
    .filter(ee.Filter.lt("CLOUDY_PIXEL_PERCENTAGE", 5))
)


def calculate_ndvi(image):
    ndvi = image.select("B8").subtract(image.select("B4")).divide(image.select("B8").add(image.select("B4")))
    return image.addBands(ndvi.rename("NDVI"))


ndvi_collection = s2_collection.map(calculate_ndvi).select("NDVI")
ndvi_params = {"min": -1, "max": 1, "palette": ["blue", "white", "green"]}
m = geemap.Map()
m.centerObject(aoi, 9)
m.addLayer(ndvi_collection.min(), ndvi_params, "Min NDVI")
m.addLayer(ndvi_collection.median(), ndvi_params, "Median NDVI")
m.addLayer(ndvi_collection.max(), ndvi_params, "Max NDVI")
m
```
  </TabItem>
</Tabs>

What the three composites tell you:

- **Maximum NDVI** shows each pixel at its greenest. It hides most clouds (clouds have low NDVI) and shows where vegetation grows at some point in the year.
- **Minimum NDVI** shows each pixel at its least green: dry season, after harvest, or under leftover cloud, snow or shadow.
- **Median NDVI** is the typical value, robust to the odd bad image.
- The **difference** between maximum and minimum separates evergreen forest (small difference) from deciduous forest and crops (large difference).

<Callout type="mistake" title="Two indices called NDWI">
The training exercise used the name NDWI for both (Green − NIR) and (NIR − SWIR-1). They measure different things: the first separates open water from land; the second tracks moisture in vegetation. Always give the formula when you report an index.

## 1.5 EVI and SAVI

Some indices have more than two bands or constants. Write them with `expression()`, which reads a formula as text and takes a dictionary that maps the names in the formula to bands:

<CodeBlock script="day-2/spectral-indices.js" lines={[132, 157]} />

- **EVI** (Enhanced Vegetation Index) corrects for some atmospheric and soil effects and saturates less than NDVI in dense forest, which is useful in Bhutan's broadleaf forests.
- **SAVI** (Soil-Adjusted Vegetation Index) adds a soil factor, L = 0.5, which helps where vegetation is sparse.

<Callout type="mistake" title="Scale the reflectance first">
Sentinel-2 surface reflectance is stored as reflectance × 10,000 (a value of 2,500 means 25%). The constants in EVI (+1) and SAVI (+0.5) assume reflectance between 0 and 1, so divide the bands by 10,000 first. The training exercise used the raw values, which gives wrong EVI and SAVI. Ratios such as NDVI and NDWI are not affected, because the scale cancels.
</Callout>

<Theory title="Normalised differences">
For bands $a$ and $b$, $ND = (a - b)/(a + b)$ always lies between −1 and 1, and any common scale factor or overall brightness change cancels. That is why NDVI is robust to illumination, but also why it saturates (stops increasing) over dense canopy, where EVI keeps responding.
</Theory>

## Key functions

| Function | What it does |
|---|---|
| `image.select('B8')` | Picks a band |
| `a.subtract(b).divide(a.add(b))` | Band maths, pixel by pixel |
| `image.normalizedDifference(['B8', 'B4'])` | (B8 − B4) / (B8 + B4) in one step; names the band `nd` |
| `image.expression(formula, {name: band})` | Computes a formula written as text |
| `image.addBands(band)` | Adds a band, keeping the others |
| `collection.map(fn).select('NDVI').min()`, `.median()`, `.max()` | Index composites |

## Common mistakes

- **Swapping the bands.** (Red − NIR) gives the negative of NDVI: forest becomes negative.
- **Forgetting `rename()`.** The index keeps the first band's name (`B8`) and later code selects the wrong band.
- **Using unscaled reflectance in EVI or SAVI.** Divide Sentinel-2 values by 10,000 first.
- **Reading maximum NDVI as "the best image".** It is assembled pixel by pixel from different dates.

<Callout type="bhutan">
Bhutan context: maximum and minimum NDVI help separate evergreen broadleaf and conifer forest (green all year) from deciduous forest, scrub and farmland (much lower in the dry season). Steep north-facing slopes are in deep shadow in winter, which lowers all indices; Day 2's terrain correction reduces this.
</Callout>
