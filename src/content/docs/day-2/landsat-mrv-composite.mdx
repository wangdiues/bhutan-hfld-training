This workflow follows the [Sentinel-2 MRV composite](/bhutan-hfld-training/day-2/sentinel-2-mrv-composite/) step for step, adapted for Landsat 8 and 9. It turns raw imagery into a stable annual product for inter-annual monitoring and model training: masking, scaling, seasonal compositing and one structured export. The aim is not to use as much data as possible, but a consistent, repeatable dataset.

**Why Landsat as well as Sentinel-2?** Landsat has 30 m pixels, but its archive goes back to the 1980s, so it can extend a time series, such as an HFLD reference period, before Sentinel-2 began in 2015. The same method, applied to Landsat 5 or 7 with their own band names (their optical bands are SR_B1–SR_B5 and SR_B7), can produce earlier years.

The workflow:

1. collect all candidate Landsat 8 and 9 images for the reporting year;
2. scale the values to surface reflectance;
3. remove cloud, cloud shadow and snow with the QA band, then remaining shadows with TDOM;
4. correct for terrain;
5. reduce each season (January–April, October–December) to a median;
6. merge the two seasons into one training-ready image.

May to September is left out, as for Sentinel-2: the monsoon makes optical data unreliable, and using those months adds gaps and bias. Both windows stay inside the reporting year.

The script reads Bhutan's boundary from `projects/bhutan-hfld-readiness-training/assets/Bhutan` and exports to the same project. Without access, use your own boundary asset and change `assetId` to a folder in your own Cloud project.
</Callout>

## Step 2: Landsat 8 and 9 together

Landsat 8 and 9 each revisit every 16 days. Merging the two gives an image every 8 days, which doubles the chance of a clear view. ## Step 3: Scale to surface reflectance

Collection 2 Level-2 bands are stored as integers. USGS gives the scale factor: **reflectance = DN × 0.0000275 − 0.2**. The function scales the six optical bands, renames them `B2`–`B7`, and keeps the `QA_PIXEL` band for masking:

<CodeBlock script="day-2/landsat-mrv-composite.js" lines={[47, 57]} />

| Landsat 8/9 band | Name | Sentinel-2 equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| B2, B3, B4 | blue, green, red | B2, B3, B4 |
| B5 | near infrared | B8 |
| B6, B7 | SWIR-1, SWIR-2 | B11, B12 |

<Callout type="mistake" title="Band numbers differ between sensors">
After this step, NIR is `B5` in the Landsat product but `B8` in the Sentinel-2 product. Don't mix the two composites' band names in one classifier.
</Callout>

## Step 4: QA masking

`QA_PIXEL` stores quality flags as bits. `bitwiseAnd(1 << n)` tests bit *n*; a pixel is kept only where all three flags are 0:

<CodeBlock script="day-2/landsat-mrv-composite.js" lines={[58, 67]} />

| Bit | Flag | Masked here? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | dilated cloud | no |
| 2 | cirrus | no |
| 3 | cloud | yes |
| 4 | cloud shadow | yes |
| 5 | snow | yes |

<Callout type="bhutan">
Bhutan context: masking bit 5 removes snow, which is common on the high peaks in January–April. Those pixels will have few or no observations in the Jan–Apr composite; that is expected. (The manual describes the mask as "cloud, shadow and cirrus"; the code masks cloud, cloud shadow and snow.)
</Callout>

## Step 5: TDOM shadow masking

QA masks miss some shadows, especially terrain shadows. TDOM finds pixels that are much darker than usual in NIR (B5) and SWIR-1 (B6), against statistics from a multi-year reference (from two years before the target year to one year after). Four parameters control it:

| Parameter | Value | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `zScoreThresh` | −1.2 | how far below normal both bands must be (lower masks less) |
| `shadowSumThresh` | 0.15 | the NIR + SWIR-1 sum must also be below this (lower masks less) |
| `contractPixels` | 2.0 | shrinks the mask to drop small, likely false patches |
| `dilatePixels` | 2.5 | grows the mask to include shadow edges |

<CodeBlock script="day-2/landsat-mrv-composite.js" lines={[68, 110]} />

These values differ from the Sentinel-2 workflow (−1.8, 0.20, 1.5, 2.5). The Landsat manual calls them tuned parameters; neither manual says how the values were chosen, so check the preview layers if you change them.

## Step 6: SCS+C terrain correction

Slopes facing away from the sun look darker and can be mistaken for a different land cover. The **Sun-Canopy-Sensor + C** (SCS+C) method uses the [SRTM DEM](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/USGS_SRTMGL1_003). It computes the illumination condition (IC) from the sun's elevation and azimuth and each pixel's slope and aspect, fits a regression between IC and reflectance for each band (at 300 m, over slopes of at least 5°), and corrects the band. The functions are by Patrick Burns and Matt Macander.

<CodeBlock script="day-2/landsat-mrv-composite.js" lines={[112, 218]} />

<Theory title="SCS+C">
For each band, regress reflectance on IC: $\rho = a + b \cdot IC$, and set $c = a/b$. The corrected reflectance is $\rho_{corr} = \rho \, \dfrac{\cos S \cos Z + c}{IC + c}$, where $S$ is the slope and $Z$ the solar zenith angle ($90°$ minus the sun elevation). The $c$ term stops over-correction on faintly lit slopes, a known problem of the plain cosine correction.
</Theory>

## Step 7: Median, Int16 and band names

Each season is reduced to its median. Multiplying by 10,000 and converting to Int16 keeps four decimal places of reflectance in half the space of a floating-point image. The bands are renamed with the season, and both composites are shown on the map:

<CodeBlock script="day-2/landsat-mrv-composite.js" lines={[220, 246]} />

| Data type | Typical range | Possible range |
|---|---|---|
| Float (before export) | 0.0–1.0 | about −0.2 to 1.6 |
| Int16 (exported) | 0–10,000 | about −2,000 to 16,000 (within Int16's −32,768 to 32,767) |

```python
composites = []
for season, (start, end) in seasons.items():
    coll = get_landsat_collection(start, end).map(scale_landsat).map(mask_landsat)
    coll = shadow_mask(coll).map(illumination_condition).map(illumination_correction)
    composite = coll.median().multiply(10000).toInt16().select(bands)
    composites.append(composite.rename([f"{b}_{season}" for b in bands]))
export_image = composites[0].addBands(composites[1])
```

## Common mistakes

- **Forgetting the scale factor.** Unscaled Collection 2 values (around 7,000–20,000) give wrong indices and thresholds.
- **Using the Sentinel-2 band names.** NIR is `B5` here.
- **Changing the windows between years.** The training distribution changes with them.
- **Masking cirrus by accident or not at all.** Check which QA bits you test; the comment and the code should agree.
- **An export name that doesn't match the year.** Build names from `targetYear`, as the course script now does.
