## Where Bhutan stands

<Term id="art-trees" /> credits jurisdictions for reducing emissions from deforestation and forest degradation. Bhutan began with a TREES Concept. On 7 April 2026, ART accepted Bhutan's TREES Registration Document and its first TREES Monitoring Report, for the **2020–2024 crediting period** <Cite id="art2026bhutan" />. Acceptance means the documents are complete and can go to independent validation and verification.

Which version of the standard applies? ART's announcement does not say. When the documents were submitted and accepted, the standard in force was **TREES 2.0** <Cite id="trees2" />; TREES 3.0 was approved two months later, in June 2026. When a new version is approved, a participant may either finish its current crediting period under the version in place when its Registration Document was accepted, adopting the latest version at the start of the next period, or start a new crediting period at once under the new version <Cite id="trees3" at="Section 1.2.2" />. From this we **infer**:

- the **2020–2024** results follow TREES 2.0;
- the **next crediting period** will follow **TREES 3.0**. That period starts on 1 January 2025, before TREES 3.0 existed, but its Registration Document is due after its first year <Cite id="trees3" at="Section 2.3" />, by which time TREES 3.0 is the latest version.

Confirm the version for any submission with the ART Secretariat. This course teaches TREES 3.0, the version Bhutan's next activity data is expected to meet; notes show where TREES 2.0 differs.

## The ART cycle in brief

1. **TREES Concept**: a short note that the jurisdiction intends to take part.
2. **TREES Registration Document**: the full description of the programme, including the forest definition, the reference period, the crediting level, the REDD+ Implementation Plan and the methods for activity data and emission factors.
3. **TREES Monitoring Report**: the results for each year, with activity data, emissions, uncertainty and safeguards.
4. **Validation and verification** by an accredited, independent body, then issuance of credits.

A **crediting period** is five calendar years, 1 January to 31 December <Cite id="trees3" at="Section 2.3" />. The **reference period** is the five calendar years immediately before it <Cite id="trees3" at="Section 4" />. After the first one, monitoring reports are due after years 1, 3 and 5 of each crediting period <Cite id="trees3" at="Section 6.2" />.

## Who counts as HFLD?

<Term id="hfld" /> jurisdictions have a lot of forest and lose little of it. Because their emissions are low, crediting them only for reductions below their past average would give them little reason to keep forests standing. TREES therefore offers them an optional **HFLD crediting approach** <Cite id="trees3" at="Section 4.2" />.

To use it, a participant must show that its **HFLD Score** is **0.5 or higher in every year of the reference period**. This is checked at the start of each crediting period, and the HFLD designation then holds for all five years <Cite id="trees3" at="Section 4.2.1" />.

<Theory title="The HFLD Score (TREES 3.0, Equations 2–4)" more="/explanation/trees-2-to-3/">
For calendar year $t$, with forest cover $FC_t$ (% of the accounting area) and deforestation rate $DR_t$ (% of the forest present that year lost that year):

$$\text{HFLD Score}_t = \underbrace{\frac{FC_t - 50}{100}}_{\text{Forest Cover Score}} + \underbrace{0.5 - DR_t}_{\text{Deforestation Rate Score}}$$

The standard's own example: forest cover 79% and deforestation 0.08% give $0.29 + 0.42 = 0.71$, above the 0.5 threshold <Cite id="trees3" at="Section 4.2.1" />.
</Theory>

<VersionNote standard="TREES 3.0" differs="TREES 2.0">
TREES 2.0 let a participant calculate an HFLD Score only if its forest cover was above 50% and its deforestation rate below 0.5% in every year of the reference period <Cite id="trees2" at="Section 5.2.1" />. TREES 3.0 removed these bounds; the 0.5 score threshold is unchanged <Cite id="trees3changes" />.
</VersionNote>

Both inputs to the score are **activity data**: the forest area each year and the area of forest lost. They come from exactly the kind of map and reference sample this course teaches you to make.

## The HFLD crediting level

The ordinary **TREES Crediting Level** is the average annual emissions over the five-year reference period, reduced by 1% (or shown by other evidence to be below business as usual) <Cite id="trees3" at="Section 4.1, Equation 1" />. The **HFLD Crediting Level** adds a small allowance for the risk of future loss:

$$HFLDCL_n = CL_n + \text{HFLD Score}_\text{avg} \times (0.05\% \text{ of the standing forest carbon stock})$$

where the carbon stock counts above- and below-ground tree biomass at the start of the crediting period <Cite id="trees3" at="Section 4.2.2, Equation 5" />. Reductions are the HFLD Crediting Level minus the year's emissions <Cite id="trees3" at="Section 10.2, Equation 11" />.

Two safeguards keep this honest:

- At the start of each crediting period, the participant must give quantitative evidence that the HFLD Crediting Level is conservative compared with the threat from the drivers of deforestation and degradation <Cite id="trees3" at="Section 4.2.2" />.
- Each year, if emissions rise above the ordinary TREES Crediting Level, a deduction applies: none up to 15% above, then 15%, 25% and 35% of the credits in steps, and **no credits** if emissions are more than 75% above it <Cite id="trees3" at="Section 4.2.2, Table 1; Section 10.2, Equations 12–13" />.

<VersionNote standard="TREES 3.0" differs="TREES 2.0">
In TREES 2.0 the TREES Crediting Level is simply the average of the reference-period emissions, with no 1% decrease <Cite id="trees2" at="Section 5.1" />. The evidence that the HFLD Crediting Level is conservative is new in TREES 3.0, which requires all crediting levels to be below business as usual <Cite id="trees3changes" />.
</VersionNote>

## What an HFLD participant must document

Among many other requirements, these are the ones the technical work in this course feeds:

**A REDD+ Implementation Plan.** It must describe the drivers of deforestation and degradation and the activities to address them. HFLD participants must also describe what they did to reduce emissions **during the reference period** <Cite id="trees3" at="Section 3.3" />. This last requirement is new in TREES 3.0 <Cite id="trees3changes" />.

**A forest definition, put into practice.** The definition must match the one Bhutan uses in its REDD+ reporting under the UNFCCC, and the **same definition must be used for the whole crediting period**. The participant must explain how the definition was made operational (for example, the canopy-cover threshold and minimum area applied in the map), and justify any difference as conservative <Cite id="trees3" at="Section 3.6" />. In this course that happens when you choose classes, training data and assemblage thresholds (Days 3–4).

<VersionNote standard="TREES 3.0" differs="TREES 2.0">
The explicit requirement to explain how the forest definition is made operational is new in TREES 3.0 <Cite id="trees3changes" />. In TREES 2.0 the forest definition is in Section 3.4 <Cite id="trees2" />.
</VersionNote>

**Activity data that a verifier can reproduce.** Activity data may come from sample-based area estimation or from verifiable ground data; areas taken directly from maps are accepted only under conditions <Cite id="trees3" at="Section 5.1.1" />. The documents must include:

- standard operating procedures for reference data collection, interpretation and sample design, training procedures and QA/QC;
- data for calendar years (prorated if necessary) and methods kept **consistent** between the reference period and the crediting period.

For **sample-based** estimates <Cite id="trees3" at="Section 5.1.1.1" />:

- report area estimates **with 90% confidence intervals**;
- use a **probability sampling design** (systematic, random or stratified random) and justify the sample size, including for small or rare classes;
- provide **the script that selected the samples** and any map used to choose them;
- have **more than one interpreter** label visually interpreted reference data;
- for a stratified design, report the error matrix, the map area of every class, and user's and producer's accuracy.

Mapped areas may be used instead of the stratified estimates only if, for each class reported, the map area lies **within the 90% confidence interval** of the stratified estimate <Cite id="trees3" at="Section 5.1.1.1" />.

<VersionNote standard="TREES 3.0" differs="TREES 2.0">
TREES 2.0 already required more than one interpreter, an accuracy assessment and confidence intervals for sample-based estimates <Cite id="trees2" at="Section 4.1.1" />. TREES 3.0 adds the explicit 90% level, the probability design, the justification of sample size for rare classes, and the sample-selection script and map.
</VersionNote>

This is the Day 5 workflow: [sample size](/bhutan-hfld-training/day-5/sample-size/), [stratified sampling in GEE](/bhutan-hfld-training/day-5/stratified-sampling-gee/), [Collect Earth Online](/bhutan-hfld-training/day-4/collect-earth-online/) and [accuracy and area](/bhutan-hfld-training/day-5/accuracy-and-area/).

## How uncertainty reduces credits

TREES expresses uncertainty as the **half-width of the 90% confidence interval as a percentage of the estimate**. It is assessed on both activity data and emission factors, and combined by Monte Carlo simulation with 10,000 runs; sampling error must be included <Cite id="trees3" at="Section 8" />. Uncertainty is assessed **separately for emission reductions and for removals**, each with its own adjustment factor (Equations 8 and 9). For emission reductions:

$$UA_{ER,t} = 0.524417 \times \frac{90\%\,CI_{ER,t}}{1.645006}$$

<Cite id="trees3" at="Section 8, Equation 8" />. The resulting **uncertainty deduction** is subtracted from the credits in the credit calculation <Cite id="trees3" at="Section 10.4.1, Equation 27" />. For example, an uncertainty of ±18.5% on the emission reductions gives an adjustment factor of $0.524417 \times 18.5 / 1.645006 \approx 5.9\%$: about 5.9% of the reductions are deducted.

<VersionNote standard="TREES 3.0" differs="TREES 2.0">
TREES 2.0 used one uncertainty adjustment and calculated the deduction within Section 8 <Cite id="trees2" at="Section 8" />. TREES 3.0 separates emission reductions from removals and moves the deduction equations to Section 10 <Cite id="trees3changes" />.
</VersionNote>

<Callout type="implication">
TREES sets no minimum map accuracy. What it rewards is **precise, unbiased area estimates**: every percentage point of uncertainty is deducted from the credits. That is why the course spends Day 5 on sample design and stratified estimation, and why the [capstone](/bhutan-hfld-training/capstone/) asks you to report uncertainty.
</Callout>

Note that the course computes the uncertainty of **areas**. In a TREES Monitoring Report, that area uncertainty is combined with the uncertainty of emission factors to give the uncertainty of the emission reductions.

## Common mistakes

<Callout type="mistake" title="Common mistakes">
- **Changing the forest definition or the method mid-period.** The definition is fixed for the crediting period, and method changes must keep the reference and crediting periods consistent, which may mean revalidating the Registration Document <Cite id="trees3" at="Sections 3.6, 5.1.1" />.
- **Reporting pixel counts as activity data.** Mapped areas are acceptable only if they fall inside the 90% confidence interval of the sample-based estimates.
- **Treating the HFLD Score as a one-off.** It must be met in every year of the reference period, and it is checked again for each crediting period.
- **Quoting a rule without its version.** Always cite the TREES version and section, and confirm which version applies to the period you report.
</Callout>

<Challenge level="complete" time={5} hints={["Forest Cover Score = (FC − 50) / 100.", "Deforestation Rate Score = 0.5 − DR, with DR in %."]}>
A jurisdiction has forest cover 70% and a deforestation rate of 0.10% in one year of its reference period. Complete: Forest Cover Score = (70 − 50) / 100 = ___; Deforestation Rate Score = 0.5 − 0.10 = ___; HFLD Score = ___. Does it meet the threshold for that year?

<Fragment slot="solution">
0.20 + 0.40 = **0.60**, which is at least 0.5, so the year meets the threshold. It must also be met in every other year of the reference period.
</Fragment>
</Challenge>

<Challenge level="blanks" time={5} hints={["UA = 0.524417 × CI / 1.645006, with CI in %."]}>
The 90% confidence interval of a year's emission reductions is ±25%. Fill in: UA = 0.524417 × ___ / 1.645006 = ___%. How many tCO₂e are deducted from 100,000 tCO₂e of reductions?

<Fragment slot="solution">
UA = 0.524417 × 25 / 1.645006 ≈ **7.97%**, so about 7,970 tCO₂e are deducted.
</Fragment>
</Challenge>
