## Image collections

An <Term id="image-collection" /> is an organised set of images, such as every Landsat 8 scene or every Sentinel-2 image. Earth Engine holds collections from Landsat, Sentinel, MODIS, Planet and many other sensors. This course mostly uses two optical satellites:

| Satellite | Operator | Since | Resolution | Revisit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Landsat | USGS / NASA | 1972 | 30 m (15 m panchromatic) | 16 days per satellite; 8 days with Landsat 8 and 9 together |
| Sentinel-2 | ESA (Copernicus) | 2015 | 10 m, 20 m and 60 m bands | 10 days per satellite; 5 days with two satellites |

The Landsat missions so far:

| Satellite | Years | Instruments | Resolution (m) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Landsat 1 | 1972–1978 | MSS | 60 |
| Landsat 2 | 1975–1982 | MSS | 60 |
| Landsat 3 | 1978–1983 | MSS | 60 |
| Landsat 4 | 1982–1993 | MSS / TM | 60 / 30 |
| Landsat 5 | 1984–2012 | MSS / TM | 60 / 30 |
| Landsat 7 | 1999–2022 (nominal orbit) | ETM+ | 30 / 15 (pan) |
| Landsat 8 | 2013–present | OLI / TIRS | 30 / 15 (pan) |
| Landsat 9 | 2021–present | OLI-2 / TIRS-2 | 30 / 15 (pan) |

### Bands and band combinations

Each sensor records several **bands**, ranges of wavelength. Landsat 8 and 9 have 11:

| Landsat 8/9 band | Name | Sentinel-2 equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | Coastal aerosol | B1 |
| B2, B3, B4 | Blue, green, red | B2, B3, B4 |
| B5 | Near infrared (NIR) | B8 |
| B6, B7 | Shortwave infrared 1 and 2 (SWIR) | B11, B12 |
| B8 | Panchromatic (15 m) | — |
| B9 | Cirrus | B10 (Level-1C only) |
| B10, B11 | Thermal infrared 1 and 2 | — |

Plus a QA band for clouds and other quality flags. Sentinel-2 also has three "red-edge" bands (B5, B6, B7) and a narrow NIR band (B8A) that Landsat lacks.

Choosing three bands for red, green and blue gives different views:

- **True colour** (Sentinel-2: B4, B3, B2) looks like a photograph.
- **False colour** (NIR, red, green; Sentinel-2: B8, B4, B3) shows healthy vegetation in bright red. It is often used for plant health.
- **Agriculture** (SWIR-1, NIR, blue; Sentinel-2: B11, B8, B2) separates crops, bare soil and forest.

## Step 1: Filter the collection

Filter by **location**, **date** and **metadata** (here, the cloud cover the data provider recorded for each image). Change the dates to the period you want, and the area and zoom to your own area of interest. The example uses Paro valley and the 2025 dry season.

<Tabs>
  <TabItem label="JavaScript (Code Editor)">
```js
var paro = ee.Geometry.Rectangle([89.25, 27.30, 89.50, 27.55]);
Map.centerObject(paro, 11);

var imageCollection = ee.ImageCollection('COPERNICUS/S2_SR_HARMONIZED')
    .filterBounds(paro)
    .filterDate('2025-01-01', '2025-05-01')
    .filter(ee.Filter.lt('CLOUDY_PIXEL_PERCENTAGE', 30));
print('Images, Jan-Apr 2025:', imageCollection.size());
```
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Python (geemap)">
```python
import ee
import geemap

ee.Initialize(project="<your-project>")  # your Earth Engine Cloud project ID

paro = ee.Geometry.Rectangle([89.25, 27.30, 89.50, 27.55])
image_collection = (
    ee.ImageCollection("COPERNICUS/S2_SR_HARMONIZED")
    .filterBounds(paro)
    .filterDate("2025-01-01", "2025-05-01")
    .filter(ee.Filter.lt("CLOUDY_PIXEL_PERCENTAGE", 30))
)
print("Images, Jan-Apr 2025:", image_collection.size().getInfo())
```
  </TabItem>
</Tabs>

You can draw the area instead: use the rectangle tool and Earth Engine adds a `geometry` import, as in the screenshot below.

</Callout>

## Step 2: Visualise images

Screens show 8-bit images: three channels (red, green, blue), each with values from 0 to 255. Visualisation parameters say how to turn your data into those values:

| Parameter | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `bands` | one band (shown in grey or with a palette) or three bands (red, green, blue) |
| `min` | the data value shown as 0 (dark) |
| `max` | the data value shown as 255 (bright) |
| `palette` | colours to stretch linearly between `min` and `max`, for one band |
| `opacity` | from 0 (transparent) to 1 (opaque) |

Use them in `Map.addLayer()`, or bake them into an RGB image with `visualize()`:

```js
var img = imageCollection.sort('CLOUDY_PIXEL_PERCENTAGE').first();
var trueColor = img.visualize({
  bands: ['B4', 'B3', 'B2'],
  min: 100,
  max: 2000
});
var falseColor = img.visualize({
  bands: ['B11', 'B8', 'B2'],
  min: [100, 75, 50],
  max: [3500, 3400, 3300]
});
Map.addLayer(trueColor, {}, 'True colour');
Map.addLayer(falseColor, {}, 'Agriculture (SWIR-1, NIR, blue)', false);
```

`min` and `max` can be one value for all bands, or one per band. With a **palette**, a single band is stretched across colours. Here elevation from 200 m to 2,500 m runs from yellow through red to blue; anything higher than 2,500 m is shown in the last colour. Data: [SRTM 30 m](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/USGS_SRTMGL1_003).

```js
var elevation = ee.Image('USGS/SRTMGL1_003');
Map.addLayer(elevation.clip(paro), {
  min: 200,
  max: 2500,
  palette: ['#ffe74c', '#ff5964', '#38618c']
}, 'Elevation (m)', false);
```

## Step 3: Reduce a collection to one image

A <Term id="reducer" /> combines many values into fewer. Reducers answer two questions: **where do the values come from and where do the results go** (`ImageCollection.reduce`, `Image.reduceRegion`, `Image.reduceNeighborhood` and others), and **how are the values combined** (`mean`, `median`, `min`, `max`, `percentile`, `linearRegression` and others).

An **image-collection reducer** reduces a stack of images to one image, pixel by pixel. The median of a few months of images removes most clouds and shadows, because they are rarely in the same place twice:

```js
var median = imageCollection
    .reduce(ee.Reducer.median(), 2);
print('Band names after reduce():', median.bandNames());
Map.addLayer(median.clip(paro), {bands: ['B4_median', 'B3_median', 'B2_median'], min: 100, max: 2000},
    'Median, Jan-Apr 2025');
```

<Image src={median} alt="Code Editor map with a median Sentinel-2 composite over part of Bhutan; snow and cloud appear white." />

`reduce()` adds the reducer's name to each band (`B4` becomes `B4_median`). The shortcut `imageCollection.median()` keeps the original names. The second argument, `2`, is the *parallel scale*: it lets Earth Engine use less memory per tile, which helps with large areas.

## Step 4: Reduce an image over a region

An **image reducer** over a region turns the pixels inside a geometry into numbers, such as the mean of each band:

<Tabs>
  <TabItem label="JavaScript (Code Editor)">
```js
var mean = median.reduceRegion({
  reducer: ee.Reducer.mean(),
  geometry: paro,
  scale: 30,
  maxPixels: 1e9,
});
print('Mean band values over Paro valley:', mean);
```
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Python (geemap)">
```python
median = image_collection.reduce(ee.Reducer.median(), 2)
mean = median.reduceRegion(reducer=ee.Reducer.mean(), geometry=paro, scale=30, maxPixels=1e9)
print("Mean band values over Paro valley:", mean.getInfo())

m = geemap.Map()
m.centerObject(paro, 11)
m.addLayer(median.clip(paro), {"bands": ["B4_median", "B3_median", "B2_median"], "min": 100, "max": 2000}, "Median")
m
```
  </TabItem>
</Tabs>

- `scale` is the pixel size in metres used for the calculation. Use the data's native resolution (10 m for most Sentinel-2 bands) for accuracy, or larger to run faster.
- `maxPixels` raises the default limit on how many pixels may be read. Set it high for large areas.

<Theory title="Image vs. collection vs. feature">
An **image** has any number of bands, each with a name and properties, plus metadata. A **geometry** is a point, line or polygon; a **feature** is a geometry plus attributes. A **collection** is a set of images or features. Reducers move between them: collection → image (`reduce`), image → numbers (`reduceRegion`), image → features (`reduceToVectors`).
</Theory>

## Key functions

| Function | What it does |
|---|---|
| `.filterBounds(geometry)` | Keeps images that intersect the geometry |
| `.filterDate(start, end)` | Keeps images from start (included) to end (excluded) |
| `.filter(ee.Filter.lt(property, value))` | Keeps images whose property is below a value |
| `image.visualize({bands, min, max, palette})` | Makes an 8-bit RGB image for display |
| `collection.reduce(ee.Reducer.median())` | Reduces a collection to one image, pixel by pixel |
| `image.reduceRegion({reducer, geometry, scale})` | Computes statistics of an image within a region |

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing up band names between sensors.** Near infrared is `B5` in Landsat 8 but `B8` in Sentinel-2.
- **Forgetting the `_median` suffix.** After `reduce(ee.Reducer.median())`, ask for `B4_median`, not `B4`.
- **`reduceRegion` without `scale`.** Earth Engine then uses the image's default projection, which for a composite is 1 degree (about 100 km), so the results are badly wrong. Always set `scale`.
- **"Too many pixels" errors.** Raise `maxPixels`, use a larger `scale`, or a smaller region.
- **An end date that is included.** `filterDate('2025-01-01', '2025-04-30')` leaves out 30 April. Use `'2025-05-01'` as the end.

<Callout type="bhutan">
Bhutan context: the monsoon (roughly June to September) leaves few clear images, so this course builds composites from the drier months, January to April and October to December. Snow on the high peaks also stays white in a median composite: it is not cloud.
</Callout>

var paro = ee.Geometry.Rectangle([89.25, 27.30, 89.50, 27.55]);
var s2 = ee.ImageCollection('COPERNICUS/S2_SR_HARMONIZED')
