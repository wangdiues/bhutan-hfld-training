The [Satellite Embedding dataset](/lulc-mapping-training/day-4/satellite-embeddings/) gives every 10 m pixel 64 numbers per year. This lab shows how the course compares them. The vectors here are **synthetic**: they were generated to behave like real embeddings, so the numbers are for learning, not for Bhutan's real pixels. Your settings are saved in this browser only.

## Things to try

1. In Part 1, set both arrows to the same direction, then 90° apart, then opposite. What similarity do you get each time?
2. Arrows 17° apart have a similarity of about 0.956. Why does the Day 5 threshold of 0.955 correspond to such a small angle?
3. In Part 2, pick **Blue pine forest, Bumthang**. Which places come next? Why is the broadleaf forest in Trongsa less similar than the fir forest in Haa, but more similar than the paddy fields?
4. In Part 3, start at the course threshold of 0.955. Which pixel is a false alarm, and what could cause it in a real 2025 composite?
5. Raise the threshold until the selective logging in Bumthang is flagged. The lab counts it as a false alarm: why is degraded forest that is still forest not deforestation?
6. Lower the threshold to 0.9. Which false alarm disappears, and which real loss is now missed? Why does it matter that the flagged pixels go into a **stratum** that is then sampled, not straight into the reported area?

<Theory title="Similarity of unit vectors" more="/explanation/embeddings/">
Satellite embeddings have unit length, so the dot product $\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b} = \sum_{i} a_i b_i$ equals $\cos\theta$, the cosine of the angle between them <Cite id="brown2025" />. Values near 1 mean the place looks and behaves the same over the year; lower values mean it changed, or is a different kind of place. A threshold on this similarity is a fast way to find candidate change, but it can't say what changed.
</Theory>

<Callout type="mistake" title="Common mistakes">
- **Reading a low similarity as deforestation.** It shows that the pixel changed. Cloud, a different season, crop rotation or a landslide also lower it, so the course uses it only to build a stratum that is checked with reference data.
- **Comparing embeddings from different dataset versions.** Use the same collection and version for both years.
- **Using the dot product on vectors that aren't unit length.** It equals the cosine only for unit vectors; otherwise divide by both lengths.
</Callout>

The lab's maths is in `src/lib/stats/embeddings.ts`, with automated tests. The threshold rule matches the [Determining sample size](/lulc-mapping-training/day-5/sample-size/) script.
