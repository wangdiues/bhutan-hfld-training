## Why validate?

Maps contain classification errors, give no sign of over- or under-estimation, and have no uncertainty. Validation with a probability sample:

1. assesses the map's accuracy;
2. derives the uncertainty of the estimates;
3. if required, uses that information to **adjust** the area estimates.

<Callout type="mistake" title="Thresholds: check the current standard">
The training slide stated two rules of thumb: "ART-TREES typically requires forest cover maps to have accuracy > 90%" and "Verra Avoided Unplanned Deforestation requires forest cover maps to have uncertainty < 20%, and uses uncertainty to adjust deforestation estimates". This course has not yet verified either against the current standards (TREES 3.0, June 2026, and the current Verra methodology), so treat them as unconfirmed. The [ART-TREES HFLD requirements](/bhutan-hfld-training/day-1/art-trees-hfld-requirements/) lesson will give the exact TREES 3.0 provisions, which address uncertainty mainly through deductions <Cite id="trees3" at="Sections 8 and 10" />.
</Callout>

## The confusion matrix

A <Term id="confusion-matrix" /> counts the samples by map class (rows: what the sample was **classified as**) and reference class (columns: what the land cover **really is**).

<Image src={matrixImg} alt="Example confusion matrix of mapped classes (forest, non-forest, deforestation) against reference observations, with row and column totals." />

| Measure | Question | Error it reflects |
|---|---|---|
| <Term id="users-accuracy" /> | Of the samples mapped as a class, how many really are that class? | commission (how wrong is the classification?) |
| <Term id="producers-accuracy" /> | Of the samples that really are a class, how many did the map get right? | omission (how much did the map miss?) |
| Overall accuracy | What share of all samples is correct? | |
| Kappa | How much better than chance is the agreement? | |

A common reading of kappa (Landis and Koch): below 0 poor; 0–0.20 slight; 0.21–0.40 fair; 0.41–0.60 moderate; 0.61–0.80 substantial; 0.81–1.00 almost perfect. Olofsson et al. advise **against** relying on kappa for accuracy reporting <Cite id="olofsson2014" />; report user's, producer's and overall accuracy instead.

<Theory title="Stratified estimators" more="/explanation/area-estimation/">
With stratified sampling, each stratum $h$ has weight $W_h = A_h/A$. The estimated area proportion of reference class $j$ is $\hat p_{\cdot j} = \sum_h W_h \, n_{hj}/n_{h\cdot}$, the adjusted area is $\hat A_j = A\,\hat p_{\cdot j}$, and its standard error is $SE(\hat A_j) = A\sqrt{\sum_h W_h^2 \frac{\hat p_{j|h}(1-\hat p_{j|h})}{n_{h\cdot}-1}}$. The 90% confidence interval is $\hat A_j \pm 1.6449\,SE(\hat A_j)$ <Cite id="olofsson2014" />. Producer's and overall accuracy must also be weighted by $W_h$; the plain counts in the matrix are biased under stratified sampling.
</Theory>

## Area adjustment

The slide's simple example uses one class's user's accuracy:

- The map shows 2,700,000 ha of forest: 70% forest cover.
- Of 100 samples mapped as forest, 98 are forest: user's accuracy 98%.
- Adjusted forest cover: 98% × 70% = 68.6%, so about 2,646,000 ha.

This shows the idea, but it only removes **commission** errors (mapped forest that isn't). Forest that the map missed, sampled in other strata, must be added back. The full stratified estimator above does both, using every cell of the matrix. The calculator below applies it.

## Uncertainty

**Percentage uncertainty** is the half-width of the 90% confidence interval as a proportion of the estimate.

Worked example (from the slide):

- The map shows 17,000 ha of deforestation.
- 18 of 23 samples mapped as deforestation really are deforestation.
- Adjusted deforestation area (from that stratum alone): 17,000 × 18/23 = **13,304 ha**.
- The standard error, from the formula above with this one stratum: 17,000 × √((18/23)(5/23)/22) = **1,495 ha**.
- 90% confidence interval half-width: 1.6449 × 1,495 = **±2,459 ha**.
- Percentage uncertainty: 2,459 / 13,304 = **18.5%**.

<Callout type="mistake" title="Corrected: the standard error in the example">
The slide's ±2,459 ha and 18.5% are right, but it gives the standard error as 1,435 ha; the formula gives 1,495 ha (1.6449 × 1,495 = 2,459). The slide's uncertainty formula also used a t-value; for samples this size, z = 1.6449 gives practically the same result.
</Callout>

## Common mistakes

- **Using unweighted accuracy from the raw matrix.** Under stratified sampling, producer's and overall accuracy must be weighted by stratum area.
- **Reporting the mapped area** instead of the adjusted area with its confidence interval.
- **Mixing up rows and columns**: rows are map classes, columns are reference classes.
- **A stratum with no samples**: its share of the area cannot be estimated.
- **Using z = 1.96 by default.** The 90% interval (z = 1.6449) is the one used here.
