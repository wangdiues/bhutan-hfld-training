## Parts 7 and 8: The sample size calculator

Copy the stratum areas into the calculator. The training used a spreadsheet; the calculator below does the same calculations and is preloaded with Bhutan's strata report from the training. It groups the classes into five strata: three forest strata (the forest area split by expected deforestation pressure), one non-forest stratum and the deforestation stratum.

For each stratum, the calculator computes:

- **W** (weight): the stratum's share of the mapped area, 0–1;
- **p**: your educated guess of the share of deforestation you expect to find in the stratum, 0–1. For the deforestation stratum this is the expected accuracy of the deforestation layer (typically 0.5–0.8); for forest strata it is the expected rate of deforestation the map missed;
- **S** = √(p(1 − p)): the standard deviation implied by that guess;
- **W × S**.

<Theory title="Total sample size" more="/explanation/area-estimation/">
With target standard error $SE = (\text{MoE}/100) \times W_\text{defor} / z$, the total sample size is $n = \left(\sum_h W_h S_h / SE\right)^2$ <Cite id="olofsson2014" at="Eq. 13" />. A ±25% margin of error on the deforestation area at 90% confidence ($z = 1.6449$) is the course's example target.
</Theory>

## Part 9: One forest stratum

Set the forest split to 100 / 0 / 0 and the expected deforestation in Forest 1 to 0.003 (0.3%), and watch the total sample size. With the workbook's areas, the calculator gives about **6,700 samples**: far too many to interpret, which could take months.

## Part 10: Three forest strata

Now split forest into three strata, 70% / 20% / 10% of its area, with expected deforestation of 0.0005, 0.001 and 0.003. These are the calculator's defaults. The total drops to **1,800 samples**. Placing more effort where deforestation is likely to be missed (near forest edges, settlements and farmland), and less where it is unlikely, cuts the sample needed.

<Callout type="mistake" title="Numbers in the exercise text">

It was probably written with an earlier version of the sheet. This page uses the spreadsheet's formulas, which the calculator reproduces exactly; ask your trainer which inputs to use for your own design.
</Callout>

## Part 11: Allocating the samples

With the total fixed, share it between strata. Non-forest is left out: here, the accuracy of forest and deforestation matters most. Three strategies:

- **Equal allocation:** the same number in every stratum.
- **Proportional allocation:** in proportion to area. The rare deforestation stratum then gets very few samples.
- **Minimum per stratum:** every sampled stratum gets at least *m*; the rest is shared among the forest strata by area.

The "ha per sample" columns show how many hectares each sample represents. Raising the minimum spreads the samples more evenly and lowers the average hectares per sample, up to a point. Try different minimums in the calculator and choose the one that keeps the deforestation stratum well sampled without starving the forest strata.

For Part 2, add up the forest strata into one **forest** sample size and keep the **deforestation** sample size separate.

The exercise text recommended a minimum of **80** (491 / 198 / 139 samples for the three forest strata and 80 for deforestation, so **828 forest and 80 deforestation**, about 1,729 ha per sample), based on its 908-sample total. With the spreadsheet's own total of 1,800, the average hectares per sample is lowest at a minimum of about **150**, giving 990 / 390 / 270 / 150, so **1,650 forest and 150 deforestation**. [Stratified sampling in GEE](/lulc-mapping-training/day-5/stratified-sampling-gee/) uses whichever allocation you choose.

<Callout type="bhutan">
Bhutan context: forest covers most of the country and deforestation very little, so the deforestation stratum is tiny by area but critical for estimating forest change. A minimum per stratum is what gives it enough samples.
</Callout>

The same strata areas in Python (geemap), assuming `map_select` and `aoi` from the JavaScript version:

```python
import ee

ee.Initialize(project="<your-project>")  # your Earth Engine Cloud project ID

areas = ee.Image.pixelArea().divide(10000).addBands(map_select.rename("stratum")).reduceRegion(
    reducer=ee.Reducer.sum().group(groupField=1, groupName="stratum"),
    geometry=aoi, scale=30, crs="EPSG:4326", maxPixels=1e13,
)
for g in areas.get("groups").getInfo():
    print(g["stratum"], round(g["sum"], 1), "ha")
```

## Common mistakes

- **Unrealistic p values.** p = 0 or 1 gives no variance; guess sensibly from experience or a pilot sample.
- **Forgetting the deforestation stratum** in the strata report, or leaving its area at 0.
- **Proportional allocation for a rare stratum.** It gets almost no samples.
- **Changing the margin of error to make the sample small** without checking the requirement you must meet.

In the calculator, find the total sample size for a **±20%** margin of error at 90% confidence with the default strata. Then choose a minimum per stratum and write down the samples for Forest (the sum of the three forest strata) and Deforestation.

<Fragment slot="solution">
A smaller margin of error needs more samples: n grows with 1/MoE², so going from 25% to 20% multiplies it by about 1.56 (from about 1,800 to about 2,800). With a minimum of, say, 150, the deforestation stratum gets 150 samples and the forest strata share the rest by area; their sum is the Forest sample size for Part 2.
</Fragment>
