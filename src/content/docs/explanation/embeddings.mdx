The Satellite Embedding dataset gives every 10 m pixel on land a list of 64 numbers for each year since 2017. The numbers come from AlphaEarth Foundations, a model from Google DeepMind <Cite id="brown2025" />. This page explains what they are and how the course uses them.

## What an embedding is

An **embedding** is a short list of numbers that summarises something complex. The model looks at a whole year of data for a place, from optical, radar and other sources, and compresses it into 64 values. It was trained so that the 64 values keep the information needed to reconstruct those observations.

No single number has a fixed meaning such as "greenness". What matters is the pattern: **places that look and behave alike over the year get similar embeddings**. A pine forest near Bumthang and one near Haa should be close to each other, and far from a paddy field in Punakha.

## Measuring similarity

The 64 values of each pixel form a vector of length 1 (a **unit vector**). For two unit vectors $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$, the dot product

$$\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b} = \sum_{i=0}^{63} a_i b_i = \cos\theta$$

is the **cosine similarity**: 1 for vectors pointing the same way (very similar), 0 for unrelated ones and −1 for opposite ones. In Earth Engine it is a multiplication followed by a sum over the bands ([Satellite embeddings](/lulc-mapping-training/day-4/satellite-embeddings/)).

## Three uses in the course

1. **Classification.** Use the 64 bands as covariates, alone or with spectral indices. Because the embeddings already summarise a year of data, a classifier often needs fewer training samples ([Embeddings: classification and similarity](/lulc-mapping-training/day-4/embeddings-classification-similarity/)).
2. **Similarity search.** Take the embedding of a few known pixels (for example, a cardamom plantation) and map the similarity of every other pixel to them.
3. **Change.** Compare the same pixel's embeddings in two years. A similarity well below 1 marks a pixel whose land surface changed, which helps target samples for rare classes such as deforestation ([Determining sample size](/lulc-mapping-training/day-5/sample-size/)).

## Limits

- **Not directly interpretable.** You can't read a land-cover class from the numbers; you need labelled samples or reference pixels.
- **Annual.** One embedding per year: changes within a year are blended, and the year must match your reporting period.
- **Model versions.** If the model or dataset is updated, embeddings can change. Record the dataset version you used.
- **Not a substitute for reference data.** A low similarity shows that something changed, not what changed or whether it counts as deforestation. Map accuracy and areas still come from a reference sample ([Area estimation](/lulc-mapping-training/explanation/area-estimation/)).
